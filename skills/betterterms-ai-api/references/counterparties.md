# Counterparties by vendor type

Patterns, not facts. Who you talk to and what they can give away
differs by vendor type. Verify each vendor's actual policy during
research; treat the notes below as expectations to check, never as
claims to cite in a draft.

## Foundation model providers (frontier API labs)

- A named account executive or account manager is the norm once spend
  is real; below that, support staff read pricing mail but cannot move
  it.
- Levers they sell: usage commits against a discount, term length,
  ramps as spend grows, rate-limit increases, capacity or early-access
  terms.
- Watch for: minimum commits set above your tested usage, and
  use-it-or-lose-it language on unused commit. Ask about rollover.

## Cloud hyperscalers

- Enterprise agreements bundle many services; private pricing and
  committed-use discounts sit behind a rep.
- Levers they sell: committed-use discounts, enterprise discount
  programs, credits, migration funding, marketplace contract vehicles.
- Watch for: a discount tied to a commit that grows every year,
  overage priced at on-demand rates, and renewal terms that reset on
  the signature date rather than the service date.

## Inference hosts and GPU clouds

- Smaller teams and shorter deals; a sales lead or founder often
  negotiates directly and can say yes in one or two rounds.
- Levers they sell: reserved capacity, volume pricing, burst
  allowances.
- Watch for: capacity promises made in chat but missing from the
  contract. Get them written in.

## Resellers and marketplaces

- Pricing passes through from the vendor, sometimes with margin on
  top. The reseller cannot change the vendor's own terms.
- Check who holds discount authority before spending rounds: the
  reseller, the vendor, or neither. If neither, negotiate with the
  vendor directly.

## Sales roles and who can say yes

- Account executive or account manager: owns the relationship, limited
  discount authority, relays everything to a deal desk.
- Renewals manager: owns the renewal date and is often the right door
  at the 90-day point.
- Deal desk or finance: approves non-standard terms and never meets
  you. Give the rep a written case they can forward: your commit, the
  alternative you tested, your timeline. Short emails travel better
  than long ones.
