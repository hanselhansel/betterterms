# Rights by jurisdiction

Each rule cites its source and the date it was read. Recheck anything
older than 90 days before relying on it. These rules shape the
market-data and pay-band moves; the case itself is a workplace
conversation, not a legal claim.

## United States, federal: discussing wages with coworkers

Rule: Under the National Labor Relations Act, employees covered by
the Act may discuss wages with coworkers, in person, by phone, or in
writing. Employer policies that prohibit or chill wage discussions
are unlawful. The right applies whether or not a union is involved.

What it means: asking coworkers what they earn is a lawful way to
gather market data for employees the Act covers. Coverage has
limits, so treat it as a right to check during research, not a
guarantee.

Source: https://www.nlrb.gov/about-nlrb/rights-we-protect/your-rights/your-rights-to-discuss-wages (read 2026-10-03)

## California: pay scale on request and in postings (Labor Code 432.3)

Rule: An employer must give an employee, on request, the pay scale
for the position they currently hold. Employers with 15 or more
employees must include the pay scale in every job posting. An
employer may not rely on an applicant's salary history when deciding
whether to hire or what to pay.

What it means: a California user can ask HR for the pay scale of
their own position in writing, and posted ranges are market data
that is easy to cite. For a promotion case, the pay scale for the
current position anchors the band math; postings for the target
level anchor the ask.

Source: https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=432.3 (read 2026-10-03)

## California: no retaliation for asking what others are paid

Rule: Per the state Labor Commissioner's guidance on the Equal Pay
Act, an employer cannot retaliate against an employee for asking how
much other employees are paid (Labor Code 1197.5(k)(1)).

What it means: in California the user may ask peers about pay, and
gathering that market data is itself protected. Wage-discussion
protection elsewhere varies; check the user's jurisdiction.

Source: https://www.dir.ca.gov/dlse/california_equal_pay_act.htm (read 2026-10-03)

## Elsewhere

- Pay transparency and wage-discussion rules differ by country,
  state, and city. During research, check whether the user's
  jurisdiction requires posted ranges or protects wage questions,
  and record each source with the date it was read.
- If the case turns on a pay gap tied to a protected characteristic,
  that is a legal issue, not a negotiation move. Surface it to the
  user and suggest professional advice; this pack does not give
  legal advice.
