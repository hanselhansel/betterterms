# Counterparty patterns

What each kind of merchant tends to do and what changes in the exchange.
These are field patterns to set expectations, not rules. The merchant's
own written policy, held as a dated source record, always wins over
anything below.

## Large retailers

- First replies run on scripts. The first "no" is usually a filter, not
  a decision, so one calm restatement is normal.
- Expect an early courtesy credit offer. Score it against the plan and
  keep pressing for the remedy when it misses the target.
- Frontline agents work to the written policy, so quoting the published
  policy back is the strongest lever in this channel.

## Marketplaces (platform plus seller)

- Two counterparties, in order: the seller first, then the platform's
  buyer-protection case when the seller stalls or refuses.
- Keep the exchange inside the platform's own messaging and case tools
  so the record builds itself where the platform can see it.
- Platforms set their own deadlines for opening a case. Find them during
  research and put them in the plan next to the dispute window.

## Digital goods and subscriptions

- Terms often say sales are final. The ask then rests on the specific
  failure (defect, non-delivery, a double charge, a charge after
  cancelling) rather than on a change of mind.
- The account-restriction risk concentrates here: after a chargeback a
  closed account can take the whole library or service with it. Brief
  the user before any `dispute` step (see playbook step 6).
- When the disputed charge followed a cancellation attempt, the
  cancellation rules in `rights.md` may apply.

## Travel and ticketing

- First offers tend to be vouchers or credit rather than money back to
  the original payment method. A credit is a different remedy and scores
  lower unless the user wanted it.
- Queues are slow, so budget patience in days as well as rounds, and
  keep the dispute window in view while waiting.

## Small and independent merchants

- Slower and more personal. Warmth plus a clear evidence file carries
  further than formal citations.
- A card dispute hits them with real fees, so the final notice is a
  serious step. Use it only when it is true: never bluff a dispute the
  user has not approved.
