# Counterparties: who negotiates a job offer

Patterns, not facts. Who the user talks to and what they can move
differs by company type. Verify during research; treat these notes as
expectations to check, never as claims to cite in a message.

## Recruiters (talent acquisition)

- Owns the process and the candidate relationship. Delivers the
  offer, fields the counter, relays to the hiring manager and the
  compensation team.
- Can often move sign-on and start date directly. Base and level
  changes usually need a band sign-off behind them.
- Wants the close: a signed candidate is their success metric, so a
  warm, well-reasoned ask gets carried forward, not killed.
- Watch for: speed pressure ("I need an answer this week") and the
  current-comp probe. Both are standard moves, not hostility.

## Hiring managers

- Owns the seat and usually pushes hardest for the candidate they
  picked. A recruiter's "let me check" often lands on this desk.
- Can argue for a higher level, a larger sign-on, or an exception
  when they want the person badly enough.
- This is the user's future working relationship. Reasons and warmth
  travel further than pressure here.

## Compensation teams and leveling committees

- Hold the bands: base ranges by level and location, equity
  guidelines, refresh policy.
- Never meet the candidate. Give the recruiter a written case they
  can forward: the precise ask, the reasons, the real alternatives.
- Exceptions exist but need a documented reason: a competing offer,
  a posted range the offer sits under, or a level mismatch.

## External and contingent recruiters

- Paid a percentage of first-year base in many markets, so a higher
  base helps them and a fast close helps them more.
- Can share what the client pays and what other candidates took.
  Treat their numbers as a lead to verify, never as a plan fact on
  their word alone.

## Founders and small companies

- One person can often say yes in a single conversation. Usually
  cash-poor and equity-rich: base moves less, equity and title move
  more.
- Ask what the equity is worth in plain terms (shares, strike,
  vesting, last valuation) before weighing it against base.

## Who you reach

- The recruiter is the channel for everything written. The hiring
  manager is the ally for level, scope, and exceptions.
- A question the recruiter cannot answer ("what is the band", "can
  sign-on move") belongs to the person behind them. Asking is normal
  and costs nothing.
