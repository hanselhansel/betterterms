# Call script template

The document the user holds during the live conversation. Build it
from `plan.yaml` after the user commits to the numbers. Write the
resolved amounts into the user's copy so they can say them out loud.
The walk-away number never appears here: it lives in `.floor`, read
only by the gate.

Fill every <angle-bracket> marker with case detail.

## 1. Opening line

Warm, names the excitement, frames the ask as making it work
together.

> "Thanks for the offer, <name>. I am excited about <the role, the
> team, or one concrete thing>. I want to make this work for both of
> us, and there are a few parts of the package I would like to talk
> through."

## 2. The ask

One precise figure from the plan, or a bolstering range from the
target upward when the user prefers range wording.

> "Based on <the posted range, the market data, the scope of the
> role>, I was hoping for {target} base."

## 3. Reasons

Two or three, each tied to a plan fact: the posted range, market
data, the user's impact or level evidence, real competing processes.
Never an invented offer or quote.

> "<Fact one, in the user's words>. <Fact two>."

## 4. Equal options

Two or three package shapes worth the same to the user, drawn from
`plan.yaml` options.

> "If base is capped, I could get there another way: <option one,
> for example a larger sign-on>, or <option two, for example equity
> or level>. Any of those gets me where I need to be."

## 5. Answers to the five likeliest objections

Draft the user's reply to each before the call.

1. "What are you making now?" -> "I would rather focus on the value
   of this role. The posted range was <range>, and I am targeting
   the top of it." (Where a salary-history ban applies, the
   user may simply decline; see `rights.md`.)
2. "That is above our band for this level." -> "What is the band for
   this level? If base is capped, let us look at sign-on, equity, or
   level."
3. "The budget for this role is set." -> "What would need to be true
   for a higher number? Who approves exceptions?"
4. "I need an answer by <day>." -> "I want to give this proper
   thought. Can I have until <date>? I will answer by then for
   sure."
5. "We do not negotiate." -> "I understand. Which parts of the
   package do have flexibility: start date, sign-on, equity, review
   timing?"

## 6. Close: ask for it in writing

> "This all sounds close. Can you send me the updated offer in
> writing? I will review it and get back to you by <date>."

## 7. When not to ask

Skip the ask when the expected gain is small and the relationship
cost is real, for example when the offer already meets or beats the
target and the user wants to sign. Naming that out loud is part of
coaching, not a failure of nerve.
