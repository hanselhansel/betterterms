# Counterparty patterns

How cancellation desks typically behave, by company type. These are
patterns to expect, not promises. The provider's written policy,
researched per case, outranks this file.

## Streaming, media, and consumer software

- Cancel flows live in account settings; save offers appear inside
  them.
- Subscriptions billed through an app store are cancelled in the
  store account, not with the provider. Ask at intake who bills the
  user.
- Pause and downgrade tiers can substitute for leaving. Check before
  cancelling.

## Telecom and broadband

- The retention desk holds better offers than front-line support.
  Ask for it.
- Note the sign-up channel; same-medium cancellation rules apply in
  some jurisdictions (see `rights.md`).
- Equipment returns and the final bill follow the cancellation. Get
  the return steps and dates in writing.

## Gyms and memberships

- Contracts can require written notice, a form, or an in-person
  step. Read the agreement before relying on chat.
- A freeze substitutes for quitting when the user wants to keep the
  membership.
- Annual contracts can carry an early termination fee; price it into
  the stay-or-leave math.

## News and publishing

- Introductory pricing expires into a higher rate, and the save
  offer is often another introductory rate with an end date. Pin
  down what the price becomes and when.
- Ask for the retention or subscriber-services desk.

## Any provider

- Agents follow a save script: the reason for leaving, then
  escalating offers. A clear, honest reason moves it along.
- The first offer is usually not the last. One counter at the
  target is the pack's policy before cancelling.
- Promises made in chat mean little until confirmed in writing.
  Close the case on a written confirmation.
