# Counterparties: subscription vendors

Patterns by vendor type. Treat each as a lead to probe, not a fact:
verify the vendor's written policy during research before relying on a
pattern in a message.

## Streaming and media

- Self-serve downgrade and cancel controls are standard; retention
  offers, when present, appear inside the cancel flow or through chat
  support.
- Ad-supported or lower tiers give a downgrade path that keeps the
  account inside the service.
- Family and multi-user plans can reframe the ask: same price, more
  seats covered.

## Consumer software and apps

- Annual billing is the main discount lever; the month-to-month price
  is the anchor to beat.
- Student, education, and nonprofit pricing often exists but is rarely
  offered unprompted. Check the pricing page, then ask.
- When billing runs through an app store, the store is the merchant of
  record: price, cancellation, and refunds follow the store's rules,
  not the vendor's. Identify who bills before choosing a move.

## News and publications

- Introductory rates expire into a higher list price; the jump at
  renewal is the negotiation opening.
- Win-back and retention offers tend to live with support and inside
  the cancel flow.

## Memberships (gyms, clubs, boxes)

- Pause and freeze options are common plan features; check the
  membership terms before asking for a price cut.
- Contracts may carry a minimum term or a cancellation fee. Find both
  in the terms before any cancel move.

## Who you reach

- Chat and email first: written, logged, and it fits the Act turn loop.
  Phone only when the vendor is phone-only.
- Front-line agents can usually apply a published discount or a listed
  retention offer; anything past that goes to a supervisor or a
  retention desk.
